Our Story

The Phil Fessenden
Memorial Fund

Named in honor of Chief Master Sergeant Phil Fessenden, this fund was established to ensure that every dollar raised through the Annual Sporting Clays for Vets Fun Shoot flows directly to local disabled veterans and first responders — with zero detours.

Organized by Englewood Masonic Lodge No. 360 in Sarasota County, Florida, the fund works hand-in-hand with local businesses, sponsors, and volunteers so overhead stays minimal and impact stays maximal.

In His Own Words

Who Was Phil Fessenden?

Phil Fessenden served 30 years across the U.S. Navy and the U.S. Air Force, retiring as a Chief Master Sergeant — the highest enlisted rate. One of the defining moments of his career came during the Cuban Missile Crisis in October 1962, when he was a member of Light Photographic Squadron 62.

His squadron flew modified Crusader fighters on low-altitude aerial reconnaissance missions over Cuba, photographing Soviet nuclear missiles from just 60,000 feet — close enough, Fessenden recalled, to read the Camel cigarette brand on a pack a man was holding in his hand below.

"Our cameras were so good our Crusaders could take aerial photographs from 60,000 feet and read the 'Camel' cigarette brand on the pack of cigarettes a man was holding in his hand. Our squadron was all over the place during the missile crisis."

President John F. Kennedy presented Light Photographic Squadron 62 with a Presidential Unit Citation for their role capturing evidence of the Russian missiles — a pivotal moment in averting World War III.

After the Navy, Phil transitioned to the Air Force as an aviation electronics Tech-Sergeant supporting the F-4 Phantom. He served in Vietnam and the Panama Canal Zone, and retired in 1985 after three decades of service.

Phil and his wife Judy moved to Port Charlotte and later to Florida in 2000. He is survived by two adult children, Deborah and Drew.

January 22, 2017 — Rest in Honor

Source: The Sun, October 3, 2016 — "Man helped photograph Soviet missiles in Cuba" by Don Moore, Sun Correspondent.

Direct Impact

Where the Money Goes

🌀
Hurricane Ian Relief

$5,000 delivered directly to Alice Bieger, a local veteran, for Hurricane Ian storm damage repair — funded entirely through the Annual Fun Shoot.

♿
All-Terrain Wheelchairs

The fund donated off-road tracked wheelchairs enabling disabled veterans and first responders to access outdoor terrain they otherwise could not reach.

🏫
Community Support

Donations to Myakka River Elementary and other local organizations in the Englewood and Sarasota community, supporting families of veterans.

Wheelchair Program

Mobility. Freedom. Dignity.

The Phil Fessenden Memorial Fund donated all-terrain tracked wheelchairs — displayed at Bass Pro Shops and available for use — giving disabled veterans and first responders the ability to hunt, fish, and move through terrain that standard wheelchairs cannot navigate.
